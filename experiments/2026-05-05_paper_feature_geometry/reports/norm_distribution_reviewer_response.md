# Norm-distribution evidence across domains (reviewer response materials)

Generated by `scripts/norm_distribution_analysis.py` from
`2026-07-21_frame_midterm_diagnosis/results/parallel_20260721_213914/per_video_scores.parquet`
(video level) and `per_frame_scores.parquet` (frame level), raw-trained checkpoints.
No new GPU inference was needed.

Claim demonstrated: **the class--norm association inverts across domains for the
same checkpoint** — CDF fakes have *larger* norms than reals; DFD fakes have
*smaller* norms than reals. The norm is a dataset-dependent cue, not a stable one.

## Reviewer response paragraph (revised with concrete numbers)

> We agree this point should be demonstrated rather than asserted, and the
> revision adds both analyses. (1) Figure X shows the class-conditional
> feature-norm distributions per domain for our seed-42 raw-trained
> checkpoints. On CDF, fake features have systematically larger norms than real
> features (CLIP: 12.06 vs 11.17, Cohen's d = +1.58; EfficientNet: 13.06 vs
> 10.50, d = +0.86); on DFD, the *same* checkpoints place fake features at
> smaller norms than real features (CLIP: 11.18 vs 11.43, d = −0.51). The
> norm–label relationship inverts across domains for identical extractors.
> (2) The norm-only AUC P(||z||_fake > ||z||_real) is above 0.5 in all 15
> seed × backbone cells on CDF and in 14/15 on FF++, but falls below 0.5 in
> 8/15 cells on DFD (CLIP 2/5: 0.345, 0.384; DINOv2 2/5: 0.458, 0.432;
> EfficientNet 4/5: 0.392, 0.476, 0.389, 0.409). We agree with the reviewer
> that this per-seed inversion is stronger support for our thesis than the
> ~0.50 average we reported, and the revised section presents the per-seed
> values and the distribution comparison explicitly. We note one additional
> observation the analysis surfaced: for DINOv2, seed 42 the inversion appears
> already on the source domain FF++ (AUC 0.341), i.e. the norm is not even a
> within-domain-stable cue for every backbone — reinforcing that it cannot
> serve as a domain-invariant discriminant.

## Notes for the manuscript

- The norm-only AUC is the rank statistic P(||z||_fake > ||z||_real); stating
  this equivalence in the text pre-empts "why AUC and not a t-test".
- Frame-level stats (in `results/norm_distribution_stats.csv`, level="frame")
  tell the same story; the paper should state the unit used (video = mean over
  frames) to match the rest of the evaluation.
- DINOv2 norms (~47–48) live on a different scale than CLIP/EfficientNet
  (~10–14): keep panels per-backbone (as in the figure) rather than pooling.
